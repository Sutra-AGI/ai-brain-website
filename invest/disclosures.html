REGULATORY DISCLOSURES, RISK FACTORS & FIDUCIARY SAFE HARBOR

Comprehensive Mandatory Disclosures Governing Early-Stage Pre-Seed Venture Capital, Fiduciary Covenants, Capital Custody, and Liquidity Risk Protections
SECTION 1: PRIVATE PLACEMENT EXEMPTION & STATUTORY SAFE HARBOR

1.1 Non-Public Offering: The investment opportunity presented on aibrainstartup.com/invest is conducted strictly as a private bilateral placement. It does not constitute a public offering, prospectus, or invitation to the public to subscribe for securities under Section 42 of the Companies Act, 2013 or regulations promulgated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI).
1.2 Accredited Investor Exclusivity: Participation is strictly limited to accredited angel investors, qualified institutional buyers (QIBs), family offices, and corporate venture capital entities who possess sufficient technical and financial sophistication to evaluate the merits and risks of early-stage artificial intelligence technology ventures.
1.3 No Collective Investment Scheme: The Company's capital mobilization does not constitute a Collective Investment Scheme (CIS) or Alternative Investment Fund (AIF) pooling vehicle. Every investor enters into a direct bilateral contract with the Company under the India Simple Agreement for Future Equity (iSAFE) architecture.

SECTION 2: COMPREHENSIVE VENTURE RISK FACTORS

Prospective investors must carefully review and accept the following inherent risks prior to initiating wire settlement:

  • Early-Stage DeepTech Execution Risk: AI Brain is pioneering novel neuromorphic associative hypergraphs to replace high-cost vector databases. While fundamental C++ algorithmic benchmarks demonstrate sub-15ms multi-hop recall and 91% cost savings, scaling distributed hypergraph clusters across high-concurrency enterprise enterprise workloads involves substantial engineering complexity.
  • Illiquidity & Transfer Restrictions: iSAFE convertible equity instruments are privately held securities with no public trading market. Secondary sales, transfers, or assignments are restricted without the prior written consent of the Company. Investors must be prepared to hold their ownership interest until a qualified financing round (Series A), strategic trade acquisition, or public listing.
  • Subsequent Financing Dilution: Upon the closing of a future priced equity round (e.g., Series A Institutional Financing), the investor's iSAFE instrument will convert into Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS) at a 25% discount to the Series A price or based on the $4.0M USD valuation cap. Subsequent financing rounds may dilute percentage ownership.
  • Foreign Exchange & Cross-Border Regulatory Risk: For subscribers wiring capital in USD, EUR, or GBP, exchange rate fluctuations may impact net conversion values into Indian Rupees (INR) for treasury R&D expenditure. All cross-border inflows and dividend repatriations are subject to the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) and Reserve Bank of India (RBI) regulations.

SECTION 4: REVENUE-SHARE DIVIDEND LIQUIDITY MECHANISM

4.1 Quarterly Payout Protocol: In addition to perpetual equity ownership, all tiers include a contractual quarterly dividend drawn from a dedicated 10% gross top-line software revenue pool. Disbursements are wired within 15 calendar days following the close of each quarter.
4.2 3.0x to 3.5x Hurdle Satisfaction: Cash revenue-share distributions conclude automatically once the investor has received exactly 3.0x (Tiers 1–3) or 3.5x (Tier 4) of their principal capital back, while their underlying equity ownership remains permanently intact and compounding.
4.3 No Guarantee of Minimum Timeline: While financial models project hurdle completion across $250K–$1.8M ARR milestones, revenue-share distributions are contingent upon actual enterprise customer collections and billed commercial API usage.

SECTION 5: GOVERNING LAW & DISPUTE RESOLUTION

5.1 Jurisdiction: All agreements, transactions, and covenants are governed by and construed in accordance with the substantive laws of the Republic of India, including the Indian Contract Act, 1872.
5.2 Institutional Arbitration: Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this placement shall be finally settled under the provisions of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 by a sole arbitrator mutually appointed by the parties, with proceedings conducted in Jaipur or Barmer, Rajasthan, India.
